EUD Technical Team — TDR & Device Support Aug 2026 – Present

William Osler Health System  ·  Project Unify (Epic HIS Go-Live)

I joined William Osler Health System as part of Project Unify, one of Ontario's largest acute care Epic implementations, covering three hospital sites — Brampton Civic Hospital, Etobicoke General Hospital, and Peel Memorial Centre.

I started in an At the Elbow (ATE) training support role, working directly with clinical staff going through the transition from Meditech Magic to Epic. You learn a lot very quickly in that environment — every unit has different workflows, different pain points, and different comfort levels with technology. I worked across adult general surgery and dialysis nursing teams, which meant understanding not just the software but the clinical context around it.

From there I was brought onto the EUD technical team to support Technical Dress Rehearsal (TDR) testing ahead of go-live. Over 2.5 weeks I verified 200+ clinical endpoints across the three sites, documented and resolved 150+ ITSM issues, and served as the primary on-site resource at PMC — often independently managing site coverage and escalating complex device and workflow issues directly to Epic HIS representatives. It was demanding work and I thrived in it.

  • Verified 200+ clinical endpoints against Epic integration standards across BCH, EGH, and PMC
  • Identified, documented, and resolved 150+ ITSM issues over 2.5 weeks ahead of go-live milestones
  • Configured and deployed clinical-grade label and network printers using Zebra and HP administration tools
  • Served as de facto on-site lead at Peel Memorial Centre during senior specialist absences
  • Escalated 10–20 complex workflow and device issues directly to Epic HIS representatives
  • Supported ATE phase training across adult general surgery and dialysis nursing teams

Goal

Why Epic — and why I won't stop until I get there

Working inside a real Epic go-live changed how I think about what healthcare IT actually means. Before Osler, I understood Epic as a software platform. After spending weeks on the floor with clinicians, watching how a poorly configured printer or a broken workflow integration creates real friction in a high-stakes clinical environment — I understood it differently. Epic is infrastructure. It's the backbone of how hospitals function, and the people who build, implement, and improve it have a direct impact on patient care at scale.

That realization is what drives me. I want to be on the side of that work that shapes the platform itself — not just deploying it, but improving it. Epic operates at a level of complexity and consequence that very few software companies match, and that is exactly the kind of environment I want to grow in. The problems are hard, the stakes are real, and the work matters in ways that are tangible.

My background is unconventional for this path — a science degree, a computing minor, bioinformatics research, and now hands-on healthcare IT implementation experience. But I think that combination is actually useful. I can talk to clinicians, I can read technical documentation, I can troubleshoot under pressure, and I understand data. What I am building toward is a role where all of that comes together: working at Epic, contributing to a platform that millions of patients and providers depend on every day.

I am not there yet. But I am closer than I was six months ago, and I will keep moving. Every ticket I resolve, every workflow issue I escalate, every go-live I get through is a step in that direction. That is how I think about the work I am doing right now — it is not a job I am passing through, it is preparation for where I am going.
