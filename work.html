Where I've been and where I'm going

EUD Technical Team: TDR & Device Support Aug 2026 – Present

William Osler Health System  ·  Project Unify (Epic HIS Go-Live)

I joined William Osler Health System as part of Project Unify, one of Ontario's largest acute care Epic implementations, covering three hospital sites: Brampton Civic Hospital, Etobicoke General Hospital, and Peel Memorial Centre.

I started in an At the Elbow (ATE) training support role, working directly with clinical staff going through the transition from Meditech Magic to Epic. You learn a lot very quickly in that environment. Every unit has different workflows, different pain points, and different comfort levels with technology. I worked across adult general surgery and dialysis nursing teams, which meant understanding not just the software but the clinical context around it.

From there I was brought onto the EUD technical team to support Technical Dress Rehearsal (TDR) testing ahead of go-live. Over 2.5 weeks I verified over 200 clinical endpoints across the three sites, documented and resolved more than 150 ITSM issues, and configured and deployed clinical-grade label and network printers using Zebra and HP administration tools. I served as the primary on-site resource at Peel Memorial Centre, often independently managing site coverage and escalating complex device and workflow issues directly to Epic HIS representatives. It was demanding work and I thrived in it.

Goal

Why Epic, and why I won't stop until I get there

Working inside a real Epic go-live changed how I think about what healthcare IT actually means. Before Osler, I understood Epic as a software platform. After spending weeks on the floor with clinicians, watching how a poorly configured printer or a broken workflow integration creates real friction in a high-stakes clinical environment, I understood it differently. Epic is infrastructure. It is the backbone of how hospitals function, and the people who build, implement, and improve it have a direct impact on patient care at scale.

That realization is what drives me. I want to be on the side of that work that shapes the platform itself, not just deploying it, but improving it. Epic operates at a level of complexity and consequence that very few software companies match, and that is exactly the kind of environment I want to grow in. The problems are hard, the stakes are real, and the work matters in ways that are tangible.

Although typical candidates for Epic roles come from a different path, I believe my path is not only unique but serves a useful purpose and gives me a different insight and edge. I entered university in a pre-med stream and built a broad foundation across medicine and the life sciences, covering areas like human anatomy, physiology, and much more before completing my Honours Science degree with a Computing Minor. That means I understand what happens in a clinical environment not just as an observer, but as someone who has studied the science behind it. That gives me a level of context when working with clinical staff that most IT professionals simply do not have. At the same time, my computing background and my time inside a live Epic implementation mean I can speak the technical language too. I do not need to choose between understanding the clinical world and the technical one. I already operate in both.

Working at Epic has always been my end goal. My current experience inside one of Ontario's largest Epic go-lives, combined with my background spanning both medicine and technology, puts me in a strong position to get there. I am already doing the work that matters.
